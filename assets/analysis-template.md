# Evidence-grounded analysis

## What changed
State the new observation and the judgment it changes, including a downgrade.

## Evidence ledger
| ID / time / person | Observation | Source / context | Baseline / cost | Independence | Dimension | Counterinterpretation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## Competing models
For each: support, strongest contrary evidence, confidence, observable falsifier.
Models can overlap; do not normalize overlapping propositions as one distribution.

## Dynamic reconstruction
- Stable tendencies versus current state; triggered mode and switching condition.
- Fast / medium / slow time windows and missing observations.
- Behavioural change versus written intention.
- Actor, partner, and interaction contributions; reciprocal feedback loop.

## Pragmatics / narrative where relevant
Literal meaning → conversational function → cost/alternatives → history/baseline.
Experience → appraisal → meaning → revised self/action rule → subsequent behaviour.

## Remaining uncertainty
Known / likely / plausible / unknown. Strongest ordinary countermodel.
Natural future evidence or direct communication that could revise the judgment.

## Earned compressed insight
One to three sentences supported by the evidence. Optional existential meaning
must follow the mechanism; avoid flattering certainty and literary decoration.
