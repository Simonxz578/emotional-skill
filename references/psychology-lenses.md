# Psychological semantics

Use theories to specify a plausible mechanism, not to certify a hidden motive. Sources and scope are reviewed in `literature-review.md`; author/year keys below connect to its verified bibliography.

| Lens / source | Can explain | Cannot prove | Avoid when |
|---|---|---|---|
| CAPS / Mischel & Shoda (1995) | Context-sensitive if–then behaviour | A fixed module or universal trait | Context or repeated observations are missing |
| Whole Trait Theory / Fleeson (2001); Fleeson & Jayawickreme (2015) | State distributions with stable tendencies | Trait scores from a single incident | Sparse sampling is presented as a density estimate |
| Narrative identity / McAdams & McLean (2013); McLean et al. (2007) | Autobiographical reasoning and self-authored rules | The factual truth or enactment of a story | Genre/audience differences are ignored |
| Partner responsiveness / Canevello & Crocker (2010) | Feeling understood, valued, cared for; reciprocal goals | Romantic attraction or objective accuracy of perception | A courteous phrase is the only evidence |
| Intimacy process / Laurenceau et al. (1998) | Disclosure coupled with perceived responsiveness | Romantic type or disclosure always helping | Private disclosure is treated as consent or destiny |
| Autonomy support / Ryan & Deci (2000) | Choice-compatible closeness | Low affection from independence | Independence is being used to ignore a stated need |
| Investment model / Le & Agnew (2003) | Persistence alongside satisfaction, alternatives, investments | Healthy love or obligation to stay | Sunk costs are treated as moral duties |
| Secure base / Feeney & Thrush (2010) | Support for exploration | Dependence, exclusivity, or a clinical category | Support is controlling rather than responsive |
| Self-expansion / Aron et al. (1992, 2000) | Inclusion and new perspectives through closeness | Destiny or romance from shared novelty | General curiosity suffices to explain the observation |
| Mentalisation / Ong et al. (2019) | Inference through goals, appraisals, expressions | Access to someone else's actual mind | Complexity exceeds the evidence: hyper-mentalising |
| Attachment variability / Girme et al. (2018) | Relationship-specific fluctuations | A permanent attachment type from response timing | Current workload or explicit communication suffices |
| Co-rumination / Rose (2002) | Repeated problem talk that can sustain closeness and distress | A diagnosis or that all deep talk is harmful | Constructive reflection produces new action and perspective |
| Social uncertainty / FeldmanHall & Shenhav (2019) | Motivation to infer, seek reassurance, and learn | Romantic reciprocation from intrusive attention | “Limerence” is used as a diagnosis or causal certainty |
| Anger approach / Carver & Harmon-Jones (2009) | Anger sometimes organising approach/action | Aggression, competence, or loss of control | Behaviour and constraints are absent |
| Self-distancing / Ayduk & Kross (2010) | Perspective change in reflection | Avoidance, dissociation, or superior insight | Detachment is automatically praised |
| Reappraisal / McRae & Gross (2020) | Changed interpretation changing emotional response | That positive reinterpretation is accurate | Real harm is being minimised |
| Precrastination / Rosenbaum et al. (2014) | Hastened subgoal completion at a cost | A general personality or relationship motive | A motor-task result is transferred to messaging without evidence |
| Anthropomorphism / Epley et al. (2007) | Attributing humanlike mind to nonhuman agents | Broad empathy to all people | One affectionate response to a machine is generalised |
| Emotion regulation / McRae & Gross (2020) | Situation, attention, appraisal, response strategies | That regulation always helps | Strategy is inferred only from emotional appearance |
| Repair after rupture / Canevello & Crocker (2010), used as a limited bridge | Accountability, responsiveness, changed interaction | Romance or restored safety from an apology | Promises recur without change |
| Dyadic coping / Falconier et al. (2015) | Shared stress management and relationship quality associations | Causal benefit for every pair | Population associations are made into individual forecasts |

Constructive co-reflection is a descriptive contrast (broader perspective, revised rule, feasible action), not a validated diagnosis. Intrusive thinking may reflect uncertainty, attention, or many other factors; the present sources do not validate a limerence classifier. Do not pathologise literary language, remotely diagnose, or use attachment to explain every event.
