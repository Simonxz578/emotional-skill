# Narrative identity as development

Read a life story as a current organisation of memory, agency, and future possibility, not a transcript of the author's entire life. Agency, redemption, contamination, continuity, rupture, future self, autobiographical reasoning, and self-authored correction describe narrative patterns. They are not diagnoses or rankings of worth.

Track experience → appraisal → meaning making → self-model update → policy update → future behaviour. Ask what the text enables the author to do next. “I was hurt, revised how I ask for help, and later asked before withdrawing” offers more support for development than “I have grown.” Distinguish retrospective reconstruction, declared intention, observed action, and sustained change.

A redemptive story converts adversity into a claimed possibility; it does not prove adversity was necessary or beneficial. A contamination story can be an accurate account of loss, not a pathology. Refusing a hopeful ending can protect truth. Audience, genre, public self-presentation, and culture shape the narrative.

Compare dated texts for changed rules and later behaviour. Contradictory action revises the policy hypothesis while leaving the expressed aspiration real. Do not convert elegant language into high confidence or borrow another person's history. Literary expressions of death, emptiness, or repetition alone do not establish symptoms.
