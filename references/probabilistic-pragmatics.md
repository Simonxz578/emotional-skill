# Language as action

Rational Speech Act models offer a computational scaffold, not proof of a speaker's actual optimization. A literal listener L0 interprets semantic compatibility; a speaker S1 chooses an utterance with informativeness/cost tradeoffs; a pragmatic listener L1 inverts that policy with contextual priors. A schematic form is `S1(u|intent,c) proportional to exp(alpha*[log L0(intent|u,c)-cost(u,c)])` and `L1(intent|u,c) proportional to S1(u|intent,c) prior(intent|c)`.

For each utterance examine:

1. Literal content and semantic ambiguity.
2. Pragmatic function: repair, soften, invite, reassure, defer, distance, test, boundary, or leave a topic open.
3. Plausible speaker goal and current emotional state; separate both from fact.
4. Time, exposure, accountability, and opportunity cost.
5. Available lower-cost alternatives (counterfactual utterance space).
6. Conspicuous omissions, conditional on actual opportunity/knowledge.
7. Baseline style toward others and toward this person.
8. Context and social/linguistic norms.
9. Relationship history and effects on specific dimensions.
10. Which interpretation makes the utterance more expected, plus its strongest rival.

A long specific apology with responsibility and a feasible repair can support preserving the connection more than a generic polite exit. Friendship, obligation, impression management, guilt, and romance remain alternatives. Follow-through discriminates accountable repair from mere wording. A costly utterance need not be honest or uniquely romantic; its cost is contrast-specific.

Do not assume an omitted phrase was considered and rejected. Ask whether the speaker had the vocabulary, attention, and social permission to choose it. Explicit boundaries remain authoritative for behaviour; a model of hidden intentions does not override them. Give confidence and natural upgrading/downgrading evidence.
